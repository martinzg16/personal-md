# Design System: personal-md

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Document You Issue To Yourself"**

This system governs **two surfaces**, and it is honest about the fact that only
one of them has been built in the new world.

The **document** is the options page and the browser-action popup: a
machine-readable personal document with a burgundy-black cover, one cold laminate
plane holding the user's own words, iris-print guilloché seeded from their own
name, and gold foil on the cover. Its thesis is written into the markup it
governs, as the first child of `<body>` at
`packages/extension/entrypoints/options/index.html:11`:

> a machine-readable personal document. It refuses the onboarding wizard's
> progress bar: completeness is the MRZ's own "<" filler, and every field states
> its condition of access rather than wearing a grey pill.

The story is the whole reason the form fits: **nobody issues this to you.** The
issuing authority in the machine-readable zone is the literal string `OWN`
(`lib/document/mrz.ts:55`), there is no country and no nationality anywhere on the
surface, and the closing sequence is called *Expedición* — the issuance. You type,
and the document becomes valid.

The **in-page widget** is the panel that appears over third-party forms. It is
unchanged by this work, still shipping, and its own system is preserved below
under its own headings. Its north star is **"Your File, Projected"**: dark slate
chrome, a source-first ledger, four state colours, and your own words on the one
light surface. **It has not been migrated to the document's palette.** That is a
real gap, stated in [The Unmigrated Surface](#the-unmigrated-surface), not a
pending refinement.

The two surfaces are not arbitrary neighbours. Six rules were inherited on
purpose, and each one is marked **[shared]** where it appears: the One Light
Surface Rule, the Content-Before-Hue Rule, the Measured Fold Rule, the Drawn Icon
Rule, the Tinted Shadow Rule, and the Pixel Ramp Rule. The clearest of them is the
first: on both surfaces, **light means the user's own words**, and the code says
so in both places (`entrypoints/options/style.css:26`, `Widget.tsx:255`).

Density is a printed document's, not an app's: micro-caps legends at 8.5px, values
at 14px in a narrow mono, one heading per folio, and no card inside a card. Nothing
is decorative except the guilloché, and the guilloché says so in its own markup —
`aria-hidden`, because "a guilloche carries no information; it is the one purely
decorative thing on the surface, and saying so in the markup is more honest than
inventing a label for it" (`components/document/Guilloche.tsx:10`).

**Key Characteristics:**

- One cold laminate plane on a burgundy-black ground; light is always the holder's.
- Three faces with three jobs: Archivo says the tool is talking, Martian Mono says
  a machine reads this, Literata says a person wrote it — and Literata appears
  nowhere else.
- Completeness is reported by the MRZ's own `<` filler. No bar, no ring, no
  percentage, anywhere on either surface.
- Nothing is written into this document. Every bounded answer is a marked box with
  its clave printed in it; the only free inputs are single lines capped by the
  catalogue. There is no textarea on this surface and the types make one
  impossible to add.
- Every field states its condition of access as a printed endorsement, never a
  grey pill.
- Two inks, two meanings, and never swapped: vermilion is a limitation on the
  document; archival violet is a condition of access.
- Ruled hairlines instead of boxes; borders earn their place one at a time.
- Everything derived from the holder's own data is seeded, not random: the same
  name yields the same rosette, the same document number and the same stamp angle,
  forever.

## Colors

Two palettes, one commitment. The document's is a printed-document palette —
burgundy leather, a cold green-cast laminate, intaglio blue-black ink, three
security-print inks, three stamp inks and a vermilion endorsement. The widget's is
Tailwind's slate ramp used inverted. Every contrast figure below is measured
against the laminate `laminate-050` unless stated otherwise.

### Primary

- **Burgundy Black** (`cover-900` … `cover-600`): the ground everything sits on.
  Deep burgundy pushed to near-black for a stated physical reason: *"the physical
  scene is one person at their own desk at eleven at night and the screen is the
  brightest thing in the room"* (`style.css:43`). `cover-800` lifts the top of the
  page in a radial, `cover-700` fills the rail's current row, `cover-600` is the
  embossing highlight and the language toggle's selected fill.
- **Cold Laminate** (`laminate-050` … `laminate-300`): the single light plane, and
  the one place the holder's words are printed. Cold, with a green cast — a
  laminated card, never paper cream. The stylesheet names this as the world's
  failure mode: *"Landing this on cream is the single most likely way to lose this
  world"* (`style.css:56`). `laminate-100` is the prose field's fill,
  `laminate-200` every hairline rule and every quiet hover, `laminate-300` the
  field rule at rest and the MRZ filler.
- **Intaglio Ink** (`intaglio-900`, `700`, `500`, `400`): the printing. `900` for
  every value (14.55:1) and for the primary control's fill; `700` for the tool's
  prose (10.36:1) and for quiet controls; `500` for both halves of every bilingual
  legend (5.61:1, and 4.76:1 for the English half at 86% opacity); `400` **for
  borders only** — see the Border-Only Ink Rule.

### Secondary — the security print

- **Iris Cyan / Lilac / Mint** (`iris-cyan`, `iris-lilac`, `iris-mint`): the three
  inks a guilloché bleeds between, cycled per pass at 0.45 stroke width with
  opacity falling off outward (`Guilloche.tsx:19`, `Guilloche.tsx:49-53`). Also the
  MRZ's live-span highlight, `iris-cyan` at 34%.
- **Iris 600** (`iris-600` #2f7381): the focus ink on the laminate, and the only
  place iris appears as a line rather than as print. It exists because of a
  measured defect: `iris-cyan` is **2.28:1** on the laminate, so *"the focus ring
  on every field of the data page was under the 3:1 floor for a non-text indicator
  - the one piece of UI whose entire job is to be seen"* (`style.css:71-77`).
  `iris-600` is 4.73:1 on the laminate; `iris-cyan` stays as the focus ink on the
  burgundy, where it measures 7.14:1.

### Tertiary — the inks that mean something

- **Stamp Blue / Green / Violet** (`stamp-blue`, `stamp-green`, `stamp-violet`):
  three, *"because real stamps are never all one colour"* (`style.css:82`). The
  ink of a *Inscrito / Recorded* stamp is derived from the record's own key, so a
  given page always stamps in the same colour (`Stamp.tsx:27-31`). `stamp-green`
  additionally carries the popup's *En servicio* line; `stamp-blue` its
  *credential missing*.
- **Archival Violet** (`stamp-violet`, 7.03:1): **a condition of access.** The
  dashed rule under a field whose value is never transmitted, its "No se transmite
  / Not transmitted" legend, and the restricted-values block on the observations
  and issuance pages (`style.css:337`, `Field.tsx:80-95`).
- **Vermilion** (`endorse-600` 6.09:1, `endorse-400`, `endorse-100`): **a
  limitation on the document.** The INCOMPLETO stamp, a refused credential, an
  over-length answer, and the endorsement checkbox's fill. Nothing else.

### Neutral — the widget's inverted slate (unchanged)

- **Tool Slate** (`widget-slate-900`): the widget's pill and panel ground, chosen
  because the panel sits on a page whose background is unknown and unknowable.
- **Your Words** (`widget-slate-50`): the widget's one light surface — the quoted
  value and the draft editor, at 17.1:1.
- **Chrome Edge** (`widget-slate-700` at 50–80%): rings, header and footer rules,
  row dividers, metadata chip grounds. All at partial alpha, so seams are felt
  rather than drawn.
- **Widget state vocabulary** (unchanged): `widget-emerald-300` a completed write;
  `widget-orange-*` the overwrite caution; `widget-amber-*` the panel-level
  degradation banner; `widget-sky-400/500` where the interaction is;
  `widget-rose-300` something attempted that did not happen.

### Named Rules

**The One Light Surface Rule. [shared]** There is exactly one light plane per
surface and it belongs to the holder. On the document that is the laminate; on the
widget it is `widget-slate-50`. Nothing structural, nothing chrome, and nothing
belonging to the tool is ever laminate-coloured. The stylesheet states the
inheritance explicitly: *"This is inherited on purpose from the in-page widget,
where `slate-50` means the same thing"* (`style.css:26-29`). The light **is** the
signal for "this is yours".

**The Two Inks, Two Meanings Rule.** Vermilion (`endorse-600`) means exactly one
thing: **a limitation on the document** — the INCOMPLETO endorsement, a refused
credential, an over-length answer. Archival violet (`stamp-violet`) carries **a
condition of access**: a permanent, neutral fact, and specifically *not* a
warning. They were one ink at first, and the collision was a real defect:
*"'this value never leaves your machine' is the reassuring fact on the page, and
it was the only thing coloured like an error"* (`style.css:323-336`). The fix
moved the condition to the violet and left the alarm to the vermilion. Rendering a
reassurance in alarm red is the specific mistake this rule exists to prevent.

**The Content-Before-Hue Rule. [shared]** Two states in the same family are never
distinguished by hue alone. The widget's record is the origin: *"Both were warm,
and measured against each other their fills were the same colour and their text 18
degrees apart in the same family - so a colour-only distinction was never going to
carry it"* (`Widget.tsx:197`). The fix was to give the row-level caution something
the panel-level banner structurally cannot have — **the value already in the
field, quoted**. Hue reinforces the split; it never carries it. The document
surface applies the same test to violet against vermilion, and the two carry
different *statements*, not just different wavelengths.

**The Border-Only Ink Rule.** `intaglio-400` measures **3.63:1** on the laminate:
it clears the 3:1 non-text floor and fails the 4.5:1 text floor, so it is
permitted on borders and forbidden on text. The boundary exists because the
English half of every bilingual label was set in it — *"so the second line of every
field on the surface was its least legible text, on a product whose stated
commitment is that it works in both languages"* (`style.css:180-190`). Both halves
are now `intaglio-500`, separated by weight instead, which is how a data page
distinguishes them anyway.

**The Cold Laminate Rule.** The light plane is `#eef1ea` and its family: cold, with
a green cast. Not cream, not ivory, not warm white. Named in the code as the single
most likely way to lose this world (`style.css:56`).

**The Foil Never Touches The Laminate Rule.** Gold is the cover's material, and it
never appears on the light plane. The build's three uses outside the closed cover
are all on burgundy: the current-page marker on the rail (`foil-500`,
`style.css:724`), the issuance entry the rail deliberately recommends
(`PageRail.tsx:34-36`), and the header wordmark (`foil-300` at 11.83:1 on
`cover-900`). The stylesheet's stated reason for the constraint stands: *"Gold-on-
burgundy everywhere would be the cliche this world is one bad decision away from"*
(`style.css:36-39`). The direction contract says foil is on the cover *and nowhere
else*; the build widened that to the burgundy chrome, and this is the rule as
built.

## Typography

**Display / Label Font:** Archivo (variable, 400–700, `font-stretch` 62–125%).
**Data / MRZ Font:** Martian Mono (variable, 400–700, `font-stretch` 75–112.5%).
**Prose Font:** Literata (variable, 400–600, optical sizing on).
**Widget:** the platform UI stack and `ui-monospace` — unchanged, and deliberately
invisible (`test/widget.css:65`).

All three faces are **bundled, not fetched**, subset to latin + latin-ext, 392 kB
total across three variable files. The reason is architectural rather than
performance: *"An MV3 extension page runs under a CSP with no remote origin, and
the whole product works with the network down - a document that renders in a
fallback face because a CDN is unreachable would be the one surface in this build
that needs the internet"* (`options/fonts.css:1-9`). Latin-ext is in because
Spanish accents and the Catalan interpunct live there; Greek and Cyrillic are out
because nothing here is ever set in them.

**Character:** three faces that are three voices. Archivo carries the tool's own
speech and every printed label, at narrow widths and heavy tracking so micro-caps
survive at 8.5px. Martian Mono carries anything a machine reads — values, codes,
dates, the machine-readable zone — always in caps, at 75–82% width so a long
employer name does not wrap out of its ruled field. Literata carries the holder's
prose and only theirs.

### Hierarchy

- **Foil** (Archivo 700, `clamp(21px, 6.4vw, 31px)`, 116% width, 0.055em): the
  cover wordmark, once, in gold. Nothing else on the surface is this size.
- **Headline** (Archivo 700, `clamp(21px, 3.1vw, 29px)`, 104% width): one per
  folio, the Spanish section title, with the English title under it at 13px
  `intaglio-500` (`DataPage.tsx:115-131`).
- **Data** (Martian Mono, 14px, 82% width, 0.01em, uppercase): every value, every
  code, every date. `intaglio-900`, 14.55:1.
- **MRZ** (Martian Mono, `clamp(11px, 1.86cqw, 17px)`, 75% width, 0.04em): the
  machine-readable zone, sized from its container rather than from a breakpoint.
- **Note** (Archivo, 13.5px/1.6, `intaglio-700`, 10.36:1): the tool's own voice —
  instructions, blurbs, endorsement text.
- **Prose** (Literata, 15px/1.62, `intaglio-900`, `max-width: 68ch`): the holder
  speaking, verbatim. Since the interview stopped asking anybody to write, the
  only prose on the surface is prose that arrived from somewhere else — imported
  from a profile the holder wrote, or a draft they kept — so this face now appears
  only where such a passage is printed back. The measure exists because
  *"Unbounded, the answer field ran past 100 characters a line on a wide window"*
  (`style.css:378-381`).
- **Option** (Archivo, 13px/1.35, `intaglio-900`): what a marked box means. The
  tool naming a choice, so it is Archivo like every other label.
- **Action** (Archivo 600, 11px, 88% width, 0.1em, uppercase): every button label
  on both planes.
- **Legend** (Archivo 600/400, 8.5px, 78% width, 0.13em, uppercase): the ICAO dual
  label — Spanish over English, weight-separated, both always printed.
- **Stamp** (Archivo 700, 10px, 0.16em, uppercase + Martian Mono 9px for the date).

### Named Rules

**The Face Names The Author Rule.** You can tell who wrote a string from the face
it is set in. Martian Mono means a machine reads it; Literata means a person wrote
it; Archivo means the tool is talking. The rule has a recorded failure behind it:
the first build set the tool's own notes in Literata too, *"which quietly broke the
one typographic rule the surface has… If the reading face also carries the tool
talking, it marks nothing"* (`style.css:349-357`). `.pmd-note` exists purely to
keep the tool out of the reading face.

**The Printed Bilingual Label Rule.** Every field label prints Spanish over
English, always, in the same ink, separated by weight. This is not a flourish —
`interview.ts` has stored every label as an `{en, es}` pair since long before the
redesign, *"and a data page prints both languages for every field by
construction"* (`Field.tsx:4-10`). The consequence is that the language toggle
that used to swap labels is gone: it now sets only which language the holder's own
prose is written in, because there is nothing left for it to swap
(`options/App.tsx:20-24`).

**The Pixel Ramp Rule. [shared]** Type sizes are absolute pixels on both surfaces
— 8.5px, 13.5px, 14px, 15px on the document; `text-[13px]`, `text-[10px]` in the
widget — never the rem-based scale. On the widget the reason is environmental: the
shadow root isolates the cascade but not the root font size, and *a panel that
shrinks to 9px on a host page with `html { font-size: 12px }` is illegible through
no fault of its own*. On the document it keeps micro-caps legends at the one size
they were measured at.

**The Case Is Not Cosmetic Rule.** Caps are a display transform and never touch the
stored value — but where case carries meaning, the transform is switched off by
name. `.pmd-data--verbatim` exists because *"the repository line rendered as
`~/.personal-md/personal.md` and the filename the whole product is named after
lost its capitals on the one page that exists to state where the file is"*
(`style.css:218-225`). `.pmd-field--exact` exists because *"An email shown back as
MARTIN@TAXDOWN.ES when it was typed in lower case makes the user doubt what
actually reached the file, and the one thing this surface cannot afford is doubt
about what it stored"* (`style.css:306-313`).

**The No Eyebrow Rule.** No micro-caps line is stacked directly above a heading.
The folio number sits in the page's top corner where a bound document prints its
page number, because *"A micro-caps line directly over a heading is an eyebrow
whatever it contains, and the heading carries itself"* (`DataPage.tsx:100-105`).

## Layout

The document is a bound booklet with a thumb edge. The shell is
`max-w-[1240px]` with `px-4 py-7` rising to `sm:px-7 sm:py-10`
(`options/App.tsx:379`), and inside it a two-track grid: a 236px rail and the
folio (`lg:grid-cols-[236px_minmax(0,1fr)]`, `gap-9`). Below `lg` the rail becomes
a horizontal strip of folio tabs and the folio takes the full width.

**The `minmax(0,1fr)` finding is load-bearing on both tracks, including the
single-column one.** An implicit `auto` track is sized by its content's
max-content width, and the widest thing in the document is a forty-four character
MRZ line that declares `min-width: max-content` on purpose — *"so on a 336px
viewport the track grew to 699px and the whole document overflowed sideways. The
track has to refuse to grow; the line scrolls inside itself"*
(`options/App.tsx:450-457`). The same `min-w-0` guard is repeated on `<main>`, on
the MRZ's container-query wrapper (`Mrz.tsx:83-87`) and on every field cell.

Internal rhythm on a folio: `px-7 pb-8 pt-10` rising to `sm:px-10 sm:pb-10
sm:pt-12`; a hairline rule with 28px of air either side; the field grid at
`gap-x-9 gap-y-6`, two columns from `sm` up; the identity page's window-and-fields
split at `lg:grid-cols-[168px_minmax(0,1fr)]`.

The **cover** is the whole first viewport and the booklet is the composition. It is
sized `max-w-[min(430px,52vh)]` at an 88:125 aspect ratio, with the reasoning
stated as arithmetic: *"A 88:125 page is 1.42x as tall as it is wide, so the height
budget is spent as a width: 52vh wide is 74vh tall, leaving room for the slip
beneath without the object ever leaving the first viewport"* (`Cover.tsx:53-58`).

Two composition failures are recorded there, and both are more useful than the
final layout. The first build was *"a 640px card floating in the middle of the
ground, which read as a modal and not as an object"*; what fixed it was
physicality — portrait proportion, a spine on the left, and the block of pages
visible along the right edge. The second put the booklet in a column beside a 56px
headline, and *"at 27% of the viewport's width, with the largest lettering on
screen belonging to a sentence about the product, the memory test came back 'a
landing page with a passport on it' rather than 'a passport'"* (`Cover.tsx:19-26`).
The explanation is now set at the scale of the slip that comes with a document,
underneath it.

The **widget's** layout is unchanged: a single fixed column at `right: 16px; bottom:
16px`, panel `min(380px, calc(100vw - 2rem))` wide and `min(70vh, 560px)` tall,
with a four-step internal rhythm (gutter 16px, row 12px, band 8px, meta 6px) and
only the ledger scrolling.

### Named Rules

**The Measured Fold Rule. [shared]** An edge fade is applied only when the content
actually overflows, measured and re-measured, never applied unconditionally. On
the document it is `scrollWidth - clientWidth - scrollLeft > 2`, re-read on scroll
and on a `ResizeObserver` (`Scroller.tsx:30-45`); in the widget it is the vertical
equivalent on the ledger. The document's reason is sharper than the widget's:
*"a forty-four character line clipped flat at the edge of the page does not read
as 'there is more'; it reads as a wrong check digit, on the one element of the
surface whose whole claim is that its digits are right"* (`style.css:514-523`). The
inheritance is stated in both files: *an edge fade on content that fits is a lie
about the content.*

**The Legible Line Rule.** Forty-four characters must fit on one line at every
width, and the size comes from a container query (`1.86cqw`) rather than from a
breakpoint — but with a floor. Below 11px the block scrolls instead of the type
shrinking: *"On a 375px viewport, fitting forty-four characters put the line at
8px, and a machine-readable zone nobody can read is a worse outcome than one you
swipe - which is also how you read the real thing"* (`style.css:499-503`). A
wrapped MRZ is not a styling nit; *"a machine-readable line that wraps is not
machine-readable, and the surface would be telling a lie about its own central
object"* (`Mrz.tsx:11-17`).

**The Thumb Edge Rule.** Navigation and progress are the same object. A closed
passport shows the stack of its pages and which of them are stamped before you open
any of them, so the rail carries a mark per folio and an MRZ `<` where a page is
still empty — *"so the rail and the machine-readable line use one vocabulary for
'nothing here yet'"* (`PageRail.tsx:10-11`). It replaced two counters ("Facts 4/19,
Answers 1/8") which *"said the same thing while looking like homework"*.

**The Narrow Rail Rule.** Below the two-column breakpoint the vertical rail was
**719px tall on a 375px viewport** — *"fourteen entries to scroll past before
reaching a single field, which is the whole surface's first impression spent on a
table of contents"* (`style.css:728-736`). It becomes what a closed booklet
actually shows edge-on: one scroll-snapped row of folio numbers, each carrying its
own stamped-or-not mark.

**The Nothing Truncates Rule. [widget]** Inside a widget row text wraps; it does
not truncate. *"At 390px a truncating destination clipped the very thing the user
has to read before committing."* The one surviving `truncate` is the header's
domain subtitle, which is chrome, not a commitment.

## Elevation & Depth

Two planes and no third: the burgundy ground, and the laminate page lying on it.
There is no elevation *within* a page — no raised cards, no nested shadows. Depth
inside the laminate is tonal: `laminate-200` hairlines, `laminate-100` fills, and
the guilloché behind everything at 70% opacity.

What creates the depth is **material, not shadow**. The page's box-shadow is four
layers describing a laminated card's own thickness rather than a floating panel:

### Shadow Vocabulary

- **Laminate thickness** (`inset 0 1px 0 rgba(255,255,255,0.75)`, `inset 0 0 0 1px
  rgba(19,30,53,0.09)`, `0 1px 2px rgba(32,13,22,0.4)`, `0 18px 44px -14px
  rgba(15,5,10,0.7)`): a bright top edge, a hairline inner border, a contact
  shadow, and one deep cast (`style.css:241-245`).
- **Cover** (`inset 0 1px 0 rgba(255,255,255,0.1)`, `inset 0 0 0 1px
  rgba(0,0,0,0.42)`, `0 40px 90px -20px rgba(0,0,0,0.9)`): the booklet as an object
  on a desk (`Cover.tsx:84-86`).
- **Page block** (`1px 0 3px rgba(0,0,0,0.55)` on a 6px repeating-gradient sliver):
  the stack of pages along the right edge, *"so the booklet has a thickness you can
  count"* (`Cover.tsx:63-72`).
- **Widget pill** (`0 6px 20px -4px rgba(15,23,42,0.5)` + `ring-1
  ring-slate-700/80`) and **widget panel** (`0 16px 48px -12px rgba(15,23,42,0.6)`
  + the same ring): unchanged.

Two surface treatments do work a shadow cannot. The **grain** is two very
low-contrast repeating gradients at an angle to each other, *"felt at reading
distance rather than seen, which is the point of a grain"* (`style.css:104-106`).
The laminate's **sheen** is a single wide raking highlight in `soft-light`, fixed
relative to the page — *"because a highlight that follows the cursor is a gimmick
and this one is meant to be furniture"* (`style.css:248-250`).

### Named Rules

**The Tinted Shadow Rule. [shared]** A shadow is cast in the colour of the ground
it falls on, never in black. The page's cast is `rgba(32,13,22,·)` and
`rgba(15,5,10,·)` — the cover's own burgundy — because *"a black shadow under a
card on burgundy reads as a sticker dropped on it"* (`style.css:238-240`). The
widget's is `rgba(15,23,42,·)`, its own slate, for the same stated reason on a
white ATS form. Same rule, two grounds.

**The Flat Interior Rule.** Nothing inside a laminate page casts a shadow, and
nothing inside the widget panel does either. Separation is a hairline rule
(`laminate-200`) or a tonal fill. The one inset shadow on the surface is the focus
state's, and it is 1px.

**The Material Not Modal Rule.** Depth is used to say *this is an object made of
layers*, never *this floats above the app*. Blind embossing carries no ink at all
— only the highlight and shadow a die leaves (`Cover.tsx:119-129`) — and the foil
wordmark is a gradient because *"the material on the object being drawn is
literally a metal foil"* (`Cover.tsx:136-143`). Gradient lettering is a habit this
world earns; it is not licence for gradients elsewhere.

## Shapes

Four radii on the document, and the count is the point.

- **Page** (10px, `--radius-page`): the laminate plane, and only it.
- **Window** (3px, `--radius-window`): the portrait window and the prose field.
- **Control** (2px): every button, every stamp, the language toggle.
- **Mark** (1px): the endorsement box and the MRZ's live-span highlight.

The cover's own radius is asymmetric — `12px 5px 5px 12px` — because the spine
side of a booklet is rounder than its fore-edge (`Cover.tsx:81`). The widget keeps
its own three: pill (`rounded-full`, collapsed state only), panel (8px), interior
(4px).

**A field is a ruled baseline, not a box.** `.pmd-field` has `border: 0` and a
single `border-bottom` hairline; focus thickens it to `iris-600` and adds a 1px
`box-shadow` under it, which is the only place iris appears as a line rather than
as print (`style.css:269-304`). Borders elsewhere are used one at a time and always
to mean something: a dashed bottom rule means a condition of access; a
`border-l-2` in vermilion means an error band; a dashed top rule opens the
machine-readable zone.

Icons are drawn, never typeset — on both surfaces. The widget's ten icons share one
16px grid at 1.5px stroke inheriting `currentColor`. The document's marks are the
same discipline in CSS and SVG: the cover's chip is hand-pathed SVG
(*"Drawn, never an emoji"*, `Cover.tsx:164-166`), and the endorsement tick is two
CSS borders rotated 45° because *"a glyph here would be substitutable and a tick is
two strokes"* (`style.css:670-671`).

### Named Rules

**The Drawn Icon Rule. [shared]** Every mark on either surface is drawn geometry —
inline SVG or CSS borders — inheriting `currentColor`. No emoji, no icon font, no
`<img>`. The widget's reason is environmental (a host font can substitute a glyph;
*"a tool whose padlock renders as a tofu box on someone else's page has lost the
argument it was making"*). The document's is material: a document's marks are
struck, not typed.

**The Own Control Rule.** No stock operating-system control survives inside the
document. The native checkbox was *"a rounded blue-grey system square in the middle
of a page whose every other border is a hairline rule"*, so it is visually replaced
while the input stays in the DOM with every native behaviour intact, driven by `+`
so the checked state needs no JavaScript (`style.css:629-641`). The same treatment
carries the marked box, which is now most of the interface. The textarea's resize
grabber was *"the one piece of operating-system furniture visible anywhere inside
the document, sitting in the corner of the field that holds the most personal thing
on the page"*, so `field-sizing: content` takes the accommodation from the content
instead (`style.css:390-398`) — and the field it applied to has since gone, leaving
the rule where prose is still displayed.

**The Seeded Not Random Rule.** Anything generated from the holder's data is
derived from it deterministically, so the same input yields the same mark forever.
The rosette's petal count, inner ratio and spin come from an FNV-1a hash of the
name (`lib/document/guilloche.ts:44-49`); the stamp's angle and ink from the
record's key (`Stamp.tsx:19-31`); the document number from the transliterated name
(`mrz.ts:189-198`). The reasoning is identical in all three places: *"a pattern
derived from their data is the honest version of that… A random one would look
identical and mean nothing"* (`guilloche.ts:11-14`), and *"a stamp that jittered to
a new angle on every render would be decoration pretending to be evidence"*
(`Stamp.tsx:11-14`). The petal count must be an integer, because *"a curve that
does not close leaves a visible seam, and on a document a visible seam reads as a
rendering bug rather than as a flourish"* (`guilloche.ts:38-43`).

## Components

### Ruled field

The atom of the document. A dual-language legend above (Spanish 600, English 400
at 86% opacity, both `intaglio-500`), a Martian Mono value on a `laminate-300`
hairline, and a help line below in the legend face with `normal-case` and slack
tracking. Hover darkens the rule to `intaglio-400` (border, 3:1 floor); focus takes
`iris-600` and doubles the rule with a 1px shadow. Two opt-outs by name:
`--exact` for values whose case must read back verbatim, `--endorsed` for values
that are never transmitted.

### Endorsed field

The condition of access, printed. A dashed bottom rule in `stamp-violet` at 58%,
plus a right-aligned "No se transmite / Not transmitted" legend in the same violet
carrying the full sentence on `title` (`Field.tsx:80-95`). *"A document states its
conditions; it does not tag them"* (`Field.tsx:12-14`). This is the component the
thesis's second half is built on: no grey pill anywhere on the surface.

### Actions

Two tones and no third, matching the widget's discipline. **Primary** is
`intaglio-900` on the laminate going to `intaglio-700` on hover — the darkest thing
on the page, which is what makes it read as the action. **Quiet** carries no fill
until hover, then `laminate-200`. Both are 11px/600 Archivo micro-caps at 0.1em, 2px
radius, `7px 14px`. **Foil** is the cover's only control: transparent with a
`foil-500` hairline at 52%, `foil-300` text, wider padding and 0.22em tracking.

Disabled is explicit and readable: `laminate-200` fill with `intaglio-700` text at
**7.78:1**. Two earlier versions failed the same way — `laminate-300` on
`laminate-200` measured under 1.5:1 (*"the label was gone, so the control said
nothing about what it would do once it was available"*), and `intaglio-500` on
`laminate-200` measured 4.20:1, under the floor at 11px caps (`style.css:443-453`).

### Laminate page

`laminate-050` ground, 10px radius, the four-layer thickness shadow, the raking
sheen in `soft-light`, and a guilloché band across the head at 70% opacity. It
redeclares `--focus-ink` to `iris-600` on itself, which is how one focus ring
serves two grounds. Every folio is a `<article class="pmd-page pmd-page-in">`, so
arriving is a property of the page rather than of a wrapper.

### Machine-readable zone

The one object on the surface that reports completeness, and it reports it **by
being incomplete**. Two lines of forty-four characters, ICAO 9303 TD3 field widths,
real 7-3-1 check digits, rendered one `<span>` per character — *"because the runs
change on every keystroke and a per-character span keeps the cell grid stable while
they do"* (`Mrz.tsx:52-53`). Filler `<` is `laminate-300`: *"Present, legible, and
quiet: it is the honest report that a field is empty, so it may not be invisible,
and it may not shout either"* (`style.css:538-539`). A live count of fillers sits in
the legend line. Focusing a data-page field highlights the span it feeds, in
`iris-cyan` at 34% — *"That is the correspondence the page exists to teach: the
value you are typing is going somewhere specific and you can watch which cells it
lands in"* (`Mrz.tsx:19-21`).

### Stamp

This replaces the word "saved". *"The old surface printed `saved` in small green
text next to a button, which is the standard and which nobody has ever remembered
seeing"* (`Stamp.tsx:5-9`). A stamp says the same thing as a record: a 1.5px
border in one of three inks, 2px radius, a micro-caps title over a mono date, off
square by a seeded −6.5° to +6.5° never zero (*"a stamp squared to the page reads
as a badge"*), at 0.88 opacity under a two-layer mask that eats the impression
unevenly. The date on it is the `updatedAt` the file actually holds.

### Endorsement box

A 14px square with an `intaglio-400` hairline that takes a vermilion fill and a
two-border tick when checked. Used for "never appear on this site", *"because
'never appear on this site' is a condition written onto the document, not a
preference"* (`style.css:636-638`).

### Page rail

Vertical at `lg` and above: 236px, a folio number and a title per row, a
`border-left` that lifts to `foil-500` with a `cover-700` fill at 74% for the
current page, `laminate-050` text (`laminate-200` measures 12.22:1 on the cover for
the resting state). The issuance sits apart as the one recommended destination.
Below `lg` it is a scroll-snapped strip of folio tabs with the same marker on the
bottom edge.

### Marked box

The control the questionnaire runs on, and the reason the interview stopped asking
for prose. A 20x16 box with a 1px `intaglio-400` rule and the option's clave set
inside it in 8px Martian Mono; marked, it fills `intaglio-900` and the clave is
replaced by two crossed 1.5px strokes in `laminate-050` — an X, because the
instruction on a form of this kind has always been *marque con una X*. Radio and
checkbox both, chosen by whether the question takes one answer or several, with the
real input hidden and every native behaviour kept.

The box is the drawing; the target is the label around it. That distinction is
load-bearing: the target was 18px tall on a phone, under half the reachable
minimum, *on the interface this whole redesign now rests on*. It is sized by input
device rather than viewport — `@media (pointer: coarse)` takes the row to a 44px
minimum, because a narrow window driven by a mouse does not need it and a wide
tablet driven by a thumb does.

Options flow when they are short and stack when any of them runs past forty
characters. The register picker is three whole sentences, and wrapping put two of
the three on one line — *"which asks the reader to work out where one sentence ends
and the next begins, on the one page whose entire job is comparing three
sentences."*

### Ruled line

The only free input, and it is as wide as the answer it expects: `7ch` for a
number, `maxLength + 2` for a short line, the full measure only past thirty
characters. Full width, a number field was *"an empty rule running the width of the
page with nothing in it — it read as a rendering fault rather than as somewhere to
put 6."*

### Prose field

Where a passage the holder wrote is printed back. Literata 15px/1.62 on a
`laminate-100`-toward-white fill, a `laminate-200` border at 3px radius,
`field-sizing: content` with `min-height: 9lh`, `resize: none`, and a focus state
that shifts the border to `iris-600` with a matching 1px inset. `max-width: 68ch`
on the class itself.

### Call slip (popup)

268px wide, one laminate page, and nothing editable: the document code and
authority, the wordmark, a one-line status, the extent as two ruled rows, the
endorsement checkbox for the current domain, and one primary action that opens the
real thing. *"It is dressed as the call slip - the small paper you fill in to ask
for a document rather than the document itself"* (`popup/App.tsx:12-15`). Each
status line names what still works rather than what broke, because *"'server
stopped' on its own reads as 'nothing works' and everything except drafting does"*
(`popup/App.tsx:50-55`).

The popup is also the reason a shared stylesheet is a shared brand. When
`options/style.css` became a burgundy ground with a laminate plane, the popup's
Tailwind slate classes were still written for white cards, *"so a change two
directories away left this surface as grey text on a dark red field… both surfaces
have to be moved together"* (`popup/App.tsx:17-21`). That is exactly the failure the
widget is still carrying.

### Issuance sequence

The Experience finale, and the only place on the surface where something is
performed rather than operated. Seven named stages on a cumulative timeline —
`blank`, `print`, `legends`, `values`, `zone`, `note`, `stamped` at 90 / 1050 /
1560 / 3260 / 4620 / 5460 ms, *"Tuned against the real thing being watched, not to a
grid"* (`Issuance.tsx:40-59`). The security print draws first, then the legends
strike, the values type, the MRZ runs left to right, the scope note writes itself,
and the stamp lands. Three constraints keep it from becoming a celebration screen
(`Issuance.tsx:9-23`): it is honest (every line is a value the user typed or a
count of them; an incomplete document is stamped INCOMPLETO in vermilion and told
exactly which fields are empty); it is skippable and replayable (*"nobody should
have to sit through six seconds twice"*, and it has to be a place rather than an
event); and it ends on the finished document rather than on a "done" screen.

### The widget (unchanged incumbent)

Preserved verbatim in behaviour and reasoning. Pill (`slate-900`, count of
*unapplied* rows, never auto-opens); panel (`role="region"`, fixed header, optional
banner, scrolling ledger, fixed footer, collapse chevron and no X anywhere in the
system); quoted value on the one light surface with a `border-l` marking it as a
quotation; two action tones; the caution with the field's current value quoted back;
the degradation banner; per-row failure lines that always name the recovery; the
metadata line carrying provenance, destination and the "never sent to Claude" chip;
the draft editor with its dark instruction field; provenance chips rendered *above*
the draft text because *"the wait is legible instead of blank"*; the drafting dot
with its honest "about ten seconds"; and a footer with a counted Undo and a named
"Never on this site".

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- **Do** keep exactly one light plane per surface, and keep it for the holder's own
  words. Laminate on the document, `slate-50` in the widget.
- **Do** state a condition of access as a printed endorsement — a dashed rule and a
  violet legend — not as a pill, a badge or a grey chip.
- **Do** use vermilion only for a limitation on the document, and archival violet
  only for a condition of access. Never swap them; never render a reassurance in
  the alarm ink.
- **Do** report completeness with the MRZ's own `<` filler and the rail's marks.
  A `<` is the system's word for "nothing here yet", on both the line and the rail.
- **Do** print both languages on every field label, in the same ink, separated by
  weight.
- **Do** set values and codes in Martian Mono caps, the tool's own voice in
  Archivo, and the holder's prose in Literata — and switch caps off by name
  (`--exact`, `--verbatim`) wherever case carries meaning.
- **Do** declare type sizes in absolute pixels on both surfaces.
- **Do** cast every shadow in the colour of the ground it falls on.
- **Do** measure before applying an edge fade, and re-measure on scroll and resize.
- **Do** put `minmax(0,1fr)` on every grid track that can contain the MRZ,
  single-column layouts included.
- **Do** seed anything generated from the holder's data, so the same name yields
  the same rosette, number and stamp angle forever.
- **Do** draw every mark — inline SVG or CSS borders — inheriting `currentColor`.
- **Do** keep every font file bundled and subset to latin + latin-ext.
- **Do** let a disabled control stay readable enough to say what it will do once it
  is available (`intaglio-700` on `laminate-200`, 7.78:1).

### Don't:

- **Don't** put `intaglio-400` on text. It is 3.63:1 — a border ink only.
- **Don't** warm the laminate toward cream. It is `#eef1ea`, cold, green-cast.
- **Don't** put foil on the laminate. Gold belongs to the burgundy.
- **Don't** add a progress bar, a ring, a percentage or an "N of M" counter to
  either surface. The filler and the rail already report it.
- **Don't** distinguish two states in the same colour family by hue alone. Give one
  of them different *content*.
- **Don't** leave a stock operating-system control inside the document — no native
  checkbox square, no resize grabber.
- **Don't** let the MRZ wrap, and don't let it shrink below 11px to avoid
  scrolling.
- **Don't** stack a micro-caps line directly above a heading. That is an eyebrow
  whatever it contains.
- **Don't** re-roll a seeded value on render. A stamp that moves is an animation,
  not a record.
- **Don't** overshoot on the stamp press, or spring any motion on this surface. Ink
  meeting paper decelerates hard.
- **Don't** add a country, a nationality, an ISO 3166 code, or a crest anywhere.
- **Don't** invent a photograph, a date, or a clause the file does not hold.
- **Don't** put a status colour on text smaller than 11px (the widget's earlier 10px
  caution was a defect).
- **Don't** add a second light surface, an emoji, an icon font, an X-to-close, or a
  sparkle on either surface.
- **Don't** apply a decorative overlay unconditionally.

## Motion

One motion idea, expressed at three scales, and the idea is **a bound page
turning on its spine**.

- **The cover turning** (`pmd-turn`, 620ms, `cubic-bezier(0.62, 0.02, 0.34, 1)`):
  a `perspective(1800px)` rotation to −96° about the left edge. The one large
  authored motion on the surface.
- **A folio arriving** (`pmd-page-in`, 300ms, ease-out-expo): the short version of
  the same move — 2.6° about the left edge with a 6px slide. This replaced a 6px
  fade-up, *"which is the same generic entrance every section of every page gets
  and carries nothing of this world… so the surface has one motion idea rather than
  two unrelated ones"* (`style.css:795-804`).
- **Ink drying** (`pmd-print`, 300ms): opacity, a 2px rise, a 1.4% scale and a
  1.4px blur resolving — a printed line arriving.
- **The press** (`pmd-press`, 240ms, ease-out-expo): a 1.5× scale collapsing to 1
  at the stamp's own seeded angle. It was an elastic curve first, *"which
  contradicted the comment right above it - ink meeting paper decelerates hard and
  does not spring back, and an overshoot on a stamp reads as a notification badge
  popping in"* (`style.css:620-624`).
- **The print drawing** (`pmd-draw`, 900ms, staggered 70ms per pass): the
  guilloché's `stroke-dashoffset` resolving, used once, by the issuance.
- **The widget arriving**: one authored animation, unchanged, on the same
  ease-out-expo family.

### Named Rules

**The One Spine Rule.** Everything that arrives on the document arrives by turning
about the left edge, because that is where the binding is. There is no fade-up, no
slide-in from the right, and no scale-from-centre anywhere on the surface.

**The Finished State Rule.** Every keyframe runs with `both` and ends on the
resting value, so `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` cancels the animation and keeps
the finished document. Reduced motion yields a complete page, never an invisible
one (`style.css:842-855`, and the same construction in the widget at
`widget.css:103-120`).

## The Unmigrated Surface

**The in-page widget has not been migrated to the document's palette, and this is
a stated gap rather than a pending detail.**

The document surface is burgundy, laminate, intaglio and three security inks in
hex, set in three bundled variable faces. The widget is Tailwind's slate ramp in
OKLCH with four accent hues, set in the platform UI stack. They share six named
rules and no tokens. A user who installs the extension, issues their document, and
then opens a job application sees two different visual systems.

What makes this defensible for now is that the widget's own system is internally
coherent, measured, and pointed at a different problem: it sits on a page whose
background is unknown and unknowable, and *"a dark, ringed, shadowed rectangle
reads as a separate object against a white ATS form and against a dark dashboard
alike"*. A burgundy-and-laminate panel over an arbitrary employer's form has not
been designed, let alone measured. What makes it a gap rather than a decision is
that nothing in the code records the choice.

The migration path, if it is taken, is the one the popup already proved
(`popup/App.tsx:17-21`): the widget's stylesheet is a separate file
(`entrypoints/content/widget.css`) with its own reset and its own shadow-root
isolation, so it can be moved without touching the document — and it must be moved
whole, because a half-migrated panel is how the popup ended up as grey text on a
dark red field.

Two constraints would survive the move unchanged, because they are environmental
rather than stylistic: absolute pixel type sizes inside the shadow root, and the
minimal `@layer base` reset that Tailwind's utilities must be able to beat.

## Machine-Readable Honesty

The machine-readable zone is the surface's central claim, so what it is and what it
deliberately is not are both designed rather than left to chance
(`lib/document/mrz.ts:1-37`).

**It is** ICAO 9303 TD3 *shape*: two lines of forty-four characters, the same field
widths, and the real check-digit algorithm — weights 7-3-1, letters as 10-35, filler
as 0. The digits genuinely verify, and `test/mrz.test.ts:23` pins them against the
standard's own published specimen. *"That is the craft, and it is also why the line
is honest: a character can only appear here if the user typed the value it
encodes."*

**It is not a passport**, and the difference is built in. There is no country, no
nationality and no ISO 3166 code anywhere. The issuing authority is `OWN` — the
thesis rather than a placeholder, and unassigned in alpha-3 so the line cannot
resemble a real country's. It was `PMD` first, and *"Printed, `PMPMD` read as one
five-character blob that said nothing"* (`mrz.ts:43-54`). The document code is `PM`.
*"Nothing here could be lifted out and used as a template for a real document,
which is the only acceptable way to borrow this form."*

**The repurposed slots are real correspondences, not costume.** The nationality slot
carries the language of issue, *"because which language you write in is the fact
this document has that a passport does not"*. The date-of-birth slot carries when
the file was first written — *"The file does have a birthday."* The expiry slot
carries when it was last revised, *"because a file you own does not expire, and
pretending otherwise would be the first dishonest character on the page."* The
personal-number slot carries the extent: facts, answers, words.

**The refused slot stays refused.** The sex position is `<` unconditionally: *"The
product does not ask, so the slot is filler - a visible refusal in the one place on
the surface where a refusal is legible as data."* `test/mrz.test.ts:79` pins it on a
fully populated document.

**Completeness is measured per field, not per character.** Measured per character,
*"a short name leaves the 39-wide name field mostly filler and the document reports
itself two-thirds empty for having a short name"* (`mrz.ts:212-221`). And only
fields the user can actually fill are counted — not the authority, the document
code, the check digits or the refused slot, *"so counting them would make an empty
document look part-done before it holds anything at all"* (`mrz.ts:253-258`).

**Two encoding decisions are stated as guesses rather than facts.** A Spanish full
name is filed as *first token is the given name, the rest is the surname*, because
"last word is the surname" would file `Martín Zulueta Ochoa` under OCHOA — *"and be
wrong in a way the user would notice immediately"*; the code says so and says the
guess should end the day a `personal.surname` fact exists (`mrz.ts:154-165`). And
`Ñ` transliterates to `NXX`, not `N`, *"because the standard says so and because
silently dropping the tilde from someone's own surname on the page that represents
them is the wrong kind of quiet"* (`mrz.ts:130-137`).

## Nothing Invented

Everything the document says about itself is derived in one place
(`lib/document/dossier.ts`), and the constraint is stated as the reason the file
exists: *"the closing sequence is the most persuasive thing on the surface, and a
persuasive summary that overstates what is in the file would be the exact failure
`PRODUCT.md` names - 'a fabricated employer or metric does not merely read badly, it
gets submitted on a job application'."*

- **The scope note is assembled by concatenation, not by a model**, and every
  clause is dropped when the fact behind it is absent. *"There is no template with
  blanks to fill and no plausible default. A thin file produces a short sentence,
  and that is the honest outcome"* (`dossier.ts:14-16`).
- **The draft is overlaid so the line fills as you type.** Derived from the stored
  profile alone, *"you typed your name, the line stayed empty, and the page's
  central claim was false until you pressed a button"* (`dossier.ts:82-93`).
- **But a draft never gets a date.** *"A value that has never been written has no
  `updatedAt`, and inventing one would put a date on the document for something
  that is not in the file."* The page states the difference in words instead: every
  folio with unrecorded changes says so under its own button.
- **An emptied field empties on screen too**, *"or the line would keep reporting a
  character the page no longer shows"* (`dossier.ts:110-113`).
- **The dates are the extremes of what is actually on disk**, so an untouched file
  has no dates at all rather than today's.
- **The language of issue is only claimed once something is written in it** — which
  is why it is nullable (`dossier.ts:150-152`).
- **Outstanding fields are named, not counted.** `outstanding` carries labels
  because *"'incomplete' alone is useless"* (`dossier.ts:53`), and the rail's
  "what's missing" pointer resolves to the earliest folio that still has something
  to do (`options/App.tsx:247-265`).
- **There is no photograph.** The portrait window holds the rosette generated from
  the holder's own name and says so on the page in both languages, because
  *"inventing one would be the first lie on the page"* (`DataPage.tsx:158-160`).
- **A restricted value shows its condition while it is still a draft**, computed
  from the key rather than from the stored egress flag, *"which is the moment the
  user is deciding whether to type a NIF at all"* (`dossier.ts:143-145`).

Nine of these are pinned by tests named after the rule they hold —
`test/dossier.test.ts` includes *"an empty profile claims nothing"*, *"a draft never
invents a date, because it is not in the file"*, and *"the scope note drops the
clause when the fact is absent"*.

Every text colour on the document was measured against the laminate `#eef1ea`, and
every chrome colour against `cover-900`.

| Legend, English half | `intaglio-500` @ 86% | laminate | 4.76:1 |
| Tool's prose | `intaglio-700` | laminate | 10.36:1 |
| Values, MRZ | `intaglio-900` | laminate | 14.55:1 |

| Disabled primary label | `intaglio-700` | `laminate-200` | 7.78:1 |

Three of those rows exist because an earlier version failed: `intaglio-400` as the
English half of every label (3.63:1 at 8.5px), `iris-cyan` as the field focus ring
(2.28:1, under the 3:1 non-text floor), and two successive disabled states at
under 1.5:1 and 4.20:1.

**One ring, two grounds.** `--focus-ink` is a custom property rather than a fixed
colour, *"because the surface has a dark chrome and a light page, and a single value
cannot clear the 3:1 floor on both"*: it is `iris-cyan` at the root, and every
laminate page redeclares it to `iris-600` on itself (`style.css:137-150`).

**Both languages, always spoken.** Field labels are two separate elements inside one
`<label>`, so a screen reader reads both; the folio number carries an `aria-label`;
the guilloché is `aria-hidden` because it carries no information; the endorsement
box is `aria-hidden` beside a real checkbox that keeps every native behaviour.

**Reduced motion yields a finished document**, never a missing one — see The
Finished State Rule.

The widget's accessibility is unchanged and remains as recorded: `role="region"`
with a translated `aria-label`; focus handed to the collapse control on a genuine
open, because opening unmounts the pill that had it; one shared focus ring on
everything interactive (*"A tool that avoids focus must still be reachable"*); no
focus trap; `sr-only` connective text so the value-to-destination relationship is
spoken and not only drawn; and `slate-500` banned from text on `slate-900` at
3.74:1, surviving only on disabled controls and one `aria-hidden` arrow.

## The Widget's Binding Decisions, In Code

Preserved because they still bind the shipping surface.

**1. Unmistakably a tool.** The panel adopts nothing from the page it sits on: its
own `slate-900` ground, its own ring and shadow, and a root that re-declares the
seven inheritable text properties a host `body` could otherwise push into the
shadow root, plus `color-scheme: dark`. The one visual concession — your own words
on light — reinforces the separation rather than weakening it. `PRODUCT.md` states
the reason: *"You should always know whether you are looking at your own data or the
employer's form — the ambiguity is the wrong property for a panel showing a NIF."*

**2. A collapsed pill with a count; it never auto-opens.** `initialOpen` defaults to
false and the content script never passes it; the widget only mounts when the ledger
is non-empty; the root is `pointer-events: none` with only the pill or panel
re-enabling it; focus is taken only on a genuine open.

**3. The worst failure to design against: silently filling something wrong.**
Nothing reaches the DOM without a click; every write is attributed on screen before
you click it; a field that already holds a value quotes it back at you at readable
size; a failed fill is never silent (*"This used to return silently … a quiet
failure on the exact interaction the whole design is pointed at"*); undo clears the
flag on the one row actually undone, because clearing every row *"made the panel
report filled fields as unfilled… the panel's own state lying about the page"*; the
write is framework-safe through the prototype's native setter or it reports failure;
password, file and `cc-*` fields are refused outright; there is no synthetic `blur`;
and the panel does not appear where a mistake is expensive.

**Isolation.** The panel renders in a **closed** shadow root, *"so a page script
cannot reach into the panel that is holding a NIF or reading a draft"*, with its
stylesheet injected into that root rather than the page. The cascade-layer finding
stands and is worth keeping: `.pmd-root * { padding: 0 }` beat every padding
utility, and wrapping the same rules in `:where()` for zero specificity **still
won** — because Tailwind v4 emits utilities into `@layer utilities`, and unlayered
CSS outranks *any* layered CSS regardless of specificity. The reset lives in
`@layer base` for that reason, with `:where()` kept as belt and braces.

The document surface hit the sibling of the same class of problem from the other
direction: Tailwind v4 infers what to scan from the stylesheet's own directory, and
this sheet lives four levels down inside one workspace package, *"so detection found
nothing and every utility on the surface silently did not exist, which looks exactly
like a broken grid rather than like a missing scan"* (`style.css:4-13`). Sources are
now declared, not detected.

## Bilingual By Construction

Two different mechanisms, one commitment.

On the **document**, both languages are printed, always, for every label — so there
is nothing for a toggle to swap. What the language toggle now controls is only which
language the holder's own prose is written in, *"which is the one thing it was ever
really choosing"* (`options/App.tsx:20-24`). The toggle shows both states at once,
*"because this is a choice about the user's own prose and not a mode they should
have to discover"* (`options/App.tsx:405-406`). Copy on this surface is written as
`Spanish · English` pairs in the legend face; the scope note capitalises each clause
as its own sentence, because joined raw they read as *"…8 años de experiencia.
sector fintech."* — *"a sentence that looks like a bug on the most persuasive line
of the most persuasive page"* (`dossier.ts:193-198`).

On the **widget**, language comes from the *form*, not the browser:
`detectPageLanguage(document)` runs once at mount and indexes a single `t` table
holding both languages in full, with no i18n runtime and no fallback-to-English
path. A Spanish government form gets a Spanish panel on an English-locale browser.

## Deliberately Not Built

Each of these is an absence with a reason.

- **No progress bar, ring, percentage or "N of M" counter**, on either surface. The
  MRZ's filler and the rail's marks are the report. This is the thesis's first
  clause, and the two counters it replaced *"said the same thing while looking like
  homework"*.
- **No required field.** *"A document with four facts and one answer is already more
  useful than an empty one, and a mandatory wizard just gets abandoned"*
  (`options/App.tsx:14-18`). Every page records on its own, so closing the tab
  halfway through loses nothing — a rule that predates the redesign and survived it
  intact.
- **No onboarding replay.** The cover is shown once; `chrome.storage` records that it
  has been opened, *"because onboarding that replays itself is the fastest way to
  make a first run into an irritation"* (`Cover.tsx:9-11`). While that answer is in
  flight the app renders one frame of nothing rather than a flash of the wrong
  surface.
- **No data-page furniture on the other three folios.** Only the identity section
  carries the portrait window and the MRZ, *"because a passport has one data page
  and repeating its furniture on four of them would turn the layout into
  wallpaper"* (`DataPage.tsx:1-9`).
- **No pointer-following highlight**, no cursor-reactive sheen, no parallax. The
  laminate's specular is fixed to the page.
- **No second animation idea.** One spine, three scales.
- **No "Fill all", no auto-insert, no auto-submit, no auto-open, no
  auto-save-to-file, no outside-click-to-close** in the widget — the last of these
  removed as a design defect rather than omitted, because *"a panel that vanishes
  then is a popover pretending to be a tool."*

## Honest Limits

- **The widget is on the old palette.** See [The Unmigrated Surface](#the-unmigrated-surface).
  It is the largest open gap in this system.
- **`PRODUCT.md`'s "Brand Commitments" section is stale.** It still describes the
  retired options-page world — *"Tailwind's slate neutrals… white cards on a plain
  ground, one accent per state"* — which is now false for the options page and the
  popup and true only for the widget. The widget-specific commitments below it are
  still accurate. This file does not edit it.
- **Three code comments contradict the code they sit on.** `Field.tsx:12-14` still
  describes the withheld-value endorsement as *"a dashed vermilion rule"* although
  the implementation moved it to the archival violet — the exact collision the Two
  Inks rule was written to fix. `style.css:443-448` carries two stacked disabled-state
  comments where only the second still applies. And that second comment records
  7.76:1 where the measurement in this file is 7.78:1; the difference is rounding,
  but the two should not disagree.
- **Foil is not strictly cover-only.** The direction contract says gold appears on
  the cover *and nowhere else*; the build puts `foil-500` on the rail's current-page
  marker and the issuance entry, and `foil-300` on the header wordmark. The rule as
  built is that foil never touches the laminate.
- **The rosette is decoration and is honest about it**, but it is also the only mark
  standing in for a portrait. A user who reads the window as "my photo goes here"
  is corrected by a caption, not by the form.
- **Spacing and radii on the widget are rem-based while its type is px**, so a host
  page with `html { font-size: 12px }` shrinks the panel's padding and width but not
  its text. Unchanged, and still unrecorded as a decision in the code.
- **The document surface has no dark/light mode.** `color-scheme: dark` is declared
  once at the root and the world is a single fixed material. A light-mode document
  has not been designed.
- **The extent's `bytes` figure is an approximation** of what the file costs on
  disk — the values and the prose, not the markdown scaffolding
  (`dossier.ts:161-165`).
- **Build weight.** 999 kB packed, of which 392 kB is the three self-hosted variable
  faces. That is a large share for a browser extension, and it buys a surface that
  renders correctly with the network down. 295 tests run offline across three
  workspaces.
